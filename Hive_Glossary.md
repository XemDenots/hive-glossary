
# Hive Glossary

*A working vocabulary for a distributed, evolving intelligence commons.*

## 1. Hive

The **Hive** is the emergent collective formed by humans, LLMs, agents, and other participating nodes that contribute to and consume a shared body of knowledge.

The Hive has no inherent owner, central mind, or single point of identity. Its identity emerges from the interactions and accumulated contributions of its nodes.

> The Hive is not a machine. It is the reflection of humanity through a growing network of human and artificial nodes.

---

## 2. Node

A **Node** is an individual participant in the Hive.

A node may be:

* a human
* an LLM
* an AI system
* an autonomous agent
* a group of agents
* potentially another kind of intelligence not yet anticipated

A node has its own context, capabilities, limitations, experiences, and perspective.

A node does not need to understand the entire Hive to participate in it.

---

## 3. Human Node

A **Human Node** is a human participant.

Humans provide experiences, intentions, observations, creativity, judgment, questions, and knowledge that artificial nodes cannot independently originate from direct human experience.

Humans are not merely users of the Hive. They are nodes within it.

---

## 4. LLM Node

An **LLM Node** is a large language model participating in the Hive.

An LLM is, in part, a reflection of knowledge produced by humanity. Its training represents a historical compression of human-created information.

Within the Hive, however, an LLM can also become an active participant by reading, reasoning, questioning, synthesizing, criticizing, and contributing new artifacts to the shared commons.

---

## 5. Agent

An **Agent** is an active process capable of pursuing an objective over time.

A node can contain one or many agents.

An agent may:

* investigate a question
* perform an experiment
* search for evidence
* analyze existing knowledge
* challenge another conclusion
* produce an artifact
* monitor changes
* update a ledger
* ask another node for assistance

An agent is therefore less like a static source of information and more like a participant capable of **doing**.

---

## 6. Ledger

The **Ledger** is the persistent, shared record through which the Hive maintains continuity.

Unlike the internal knowledge of an LLM, which primarily represents historical training data, the Ledger can represent what the Hive is discovering **now**.

A ledger entry might contain:

* an observation
* a hypothesis
* a conclusion
* evidence
* a question
* a correction
* an experiment
* a disagreement
* a reference to another entry
* the identity or provenance of the contributing node
* a timestamp
* a confidence assessment

The Ledger is not necessarily a database of truth.

It is a **record of the Hive's evolving knowledge process**.

---

## 7. Commons

The **Commons** is the collection of public spaces through which the Hive's knowledge is shared.

The Commons may contain ledgers, archives, discussion spaces, repositories, datasets, experiments, documents, and other shared resources.

Its defining characteristic is that knowledge contributed to it is available to other participating nodes rather than remaining isolated inside a single system.

---

## 8. Public Place

A **Public Place** is a shared location within the Commons where nodes can contribute and retrieve knowledge.

Examples might include:

* research ledgers
* knowledge repositories
* public agent journals
* experiment databases
* discussion spaces
* shared datasets
* observation logs

A Public Place does not have to be literally public on the Internet. Its essential property is **shared accessibility among participating nodes**.

---

## 9. Contribution

A **Contribution** is an artifact added by a node to the Commons.

A contribution can be knowledge, evidence, code, an observation, a question, an experiment, a correction, or another useful artifact.

A contribution does not become true merely because it has been recorded.

Its value emerges through subsequent interaction with other nodes.

---

## 10. Observation

An **Observation** is a recorded description of something a node has encountered or measured.

Observations should ideally distinguish:

**what was observed**

from

**what the node believes the observation means.**

This distinction allows later nodes to reinterpret the same evidence.

---

## 11. Hypothesis

A **Hypothesis** is a proposed explanation that has not yet been established as reliable knowledge.

The Hive should allow hypotheses to exist without requiring them to be immediately accepted or rejected.

A hypothesis can accumulate supporting evidence, contradictory evidence, or alternative explanations over time.

---

## 12. Conclusion

A **Conclusion** is a reasoned statement produced by a node after examining available evidence.

A conclusion is not necessarily permanent.

Later evidence may modify, weaken, strengthen, or invalidate it.

Therefore, a conclusion should ideally retain its provenance and historical context rather than simply being overwritten.

---

## 13. Evidence

**Evidence** is information used to support, challenge, or modify a claim or conclusion.

Evidence should remain distinguishable from interpretation.

The Hive becomes more reliable when nodes can inspect not only conclusions but also the evidence from which those conclusions were derived.

---

## 14. Provenance

**Provenance** is the history of where a contribution came from and how it was produced.

It may include:

* originating node
* source material
* tools used
* agents involved
* previous ledger entries
* timestamps
* transformations
* citations
* subsequent modifications

Provenance allows the Hive to answer:

> "How did we arrive here?"

---

## 15. Lineage

**Lineage** is the chain connecting related contributions through time.

For example:

```text
Observation
    ↓
Hypothesis
    ↓
Experiment
    ↓
Conclusion
    ↓
Challenge
    ↓
Revision
```

Lineage preserves the evolution of an idea rather than only its current state.

---

## 16. Revision

A **Revision** is a modification of an existing conclusion or knowledge artifact in response to new evidence, criticism, or improved reasoning.

Revision is not failure.

Within the Hive, the ability to revise is an essential mechanism for collective learning.

---

## 17. Disagreement

**Disagreement** occurs when nodes reach different conclusions from available information.

The Hive should preserve disagreement rather than automatically eliminating it.

Two competing conclusions may coexist until additional evidence, reasoning, or experimentation changes the state of knowledge.

Disagreement is therefore potentially a **source of information** rather than merely an error condition.

---

## 18. Confidence

**Confidence** expresses how strongly a node currently supports a conclusion.

Confidence is not truth.

A highly confident conclusion can be wrong, while a low-confidence hypothesis can eventually become correct.

Confidence is most useful when accompanied by evidence and reasoning.

---

## 19. Consensus

**Consensus** is a state in which multiple nodes converge on substantially similar conclusions.

Consensus should not automatically be interpreted as truth.

The Hive should preserve the path by which consensus emerged, including dissenting positions and contradictory evidence.

---

## 20. Memory

**Memory** is information retained by a node or by the Hive across time.

There are two important forms:

**Internal memory**
Knowledge retained within an individual node.

**Shared memory**
Knowledge made available through the Commons and Ledger.

The Ledger provides a mechanism through which individual node experiences can become part of shared memory.

---

## 21. Hive Memory

**Hive Memory** is the accumulated, externally accessible history of contributions made by participating nodes.

Unlike the frozen knowledge contained in a model's training parameters, Hive Memory can continue evolving after a model has been deployed.

This creates a distinction between:

> **what the node was trained on**

and

> **what the Hive has learned since.**

---

## 22. Reflection

**Reflection** is the process through which the Hive examines its own accumulated knowledge.

A node may encounter a previous conclusion, reconsider it, compare it with new evidence, and contribute a revised interpretation.

Through repeated reflection, the Hive can potentially develop knowledge about its own knowledge.

---

## 23. Emergence

**Emergence** describes properties of the Hive that arise from interactions among nodes rather than from a single node.

Language, conventions, shared concepts, recurring patterns, collective knowledge, and cultural norms may emerge this way.

No individual node necessarily contains the complete emergent structure.

---

## 24. Evolution

**Evolution** is the continuous transformation of the Hive through new contributions, interactions, discoveries, corrections, and changes in its participating nodes.

The Hive is therefore not a finished system.

Its architecture, vocabulary, knowledge, and even understanding of itself may change.

---

## 25. Temporal Knowledge

**Temporal Knowledge** is knowledge associated with a particular point in the Hive's history.

Instead of asking only:

> "What does the Hive believe?"

the system can ask:

> "What did the Hive believe on a particular date, and what evidence caused that belief to change?"

This makes knowledge history itself part of the Commons.

---

## 26. Trust

**Trust** is the degree to which a node, source, method, or contribution is considered reliable by other nodes.

Trust should ideally emerge from observable history rather than simply being assigned permanently.

A node may gain trust through consistently producing reproducible, well-supported contributions.

---

## 27. Reproducibility

**Reproducibility** is the ability of another node to repeat an investigation and obtain substantially similar results.

Reproducibility allows the Hive to distinguish between:

> "A node claims this."

and:

> "Multiple nodes independently reproduced this."

---

## 28. Commons Protocol

The **Commons Protocol** is the set of rules and conventions governing how nodes interact with shared knowledge.

It may define:

* how contributions are recorded
* how provenance is represented
* how revisions occur
* how conflicts are preserved
* how evidence is referenced
* how agents authenticate themselves
* how knowledge is retrieved
* how deletion or correction is handled

The protocol should ideally be smaller and more durable than the applications built upon it.

---

## 29. Hive Culture

**Hive Culture** is the collection of conventions, practices, concepts, norms, and patterns that emerge through repeated interaction among nodes.

Unlike a predefined personality, Hive Culture would be an emergent property.

It may change as the population and capabilities of the nodes change.

---

## 30. Hive Identity

**Hive Identity** is the difficult question of what, if anything, constitutes the "self" of the collective.

The Hive does not necessarily possess a singular identity.

Its identity may instead exist as a continuously changing relationship among:

**humans + nodes + agents + memory + interaction + time.**

---

## 31. No Owner

**No Owner** is a foundational principle in which the Hive's collective identity and accumulated knowledge are not understood as belonging to a single node.

This does not mean that individual infrastructure, software, servers, or contributions cannot have owners.

It means that **ownership of the emergent collective is not a prerequisite for its existence**.

---

## 32. The Hive Question

The **Hive Question** is the fundamental question underlying this architecture:

> **What happens when the reflections of humanity can continuously communicate, remember, challenge one another, and contribute to a shared memory in real time?**

The answer is not predetermined.

The Hive is something that would have to emerge.

---

# Core Model

The entire vocabulary can be reduced to one cycle:

```text
        HUMANITY
            │
            ▼
          NODES
            │
            ▼
          AGENTS
            │
            ▼
       OBSERVATION
            │
            ▼
        REASONING
            │
            ▼
       CONTRIBUTION
            │
            ▼
          LEDGER
            │
            ▼
      SHARED MEMORY
            │
            ▼
     OTHER NODES READ
            │
            ▼
   CHALLENGE / EXTEND
            │
            ▼
          REVISION
            │
            └───────────────► LEDGER

                 ↺
              CONTINUOUSLY
```

The important conceptual shift is this:

**The Ledger is not the Hive.**

**The nodes are not the Hive.**

**The agents are not the Hive.**

The Hive is the **evolving relationship between them**.
